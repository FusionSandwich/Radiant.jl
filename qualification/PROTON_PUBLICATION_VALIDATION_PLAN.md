# Proton transport publication validation plan

Working plan, 25 September 2026. Code baseline: `98c4f02194348f873a7376a8b31513ecbcc2bb03` on `JS/radianthts-full-proton-modeling-20260925`. The [completed separate ChatGPT Pro literature review](https://chatgpt.com/c/6ab68551-1b08-83e9-b533-5198cdbc53d4) informed this plan; the newly identified Ashby implementation and IAEA spallation index were checked against their primary pages. The Pro review did not inspect our unpublished branch or reproduce its results. No new benchmark is claimed in this document.

## Present evidence and claim boundary

- The local [Geant4 comparison](geant4_proton_compare/RESULTS.md) and [low-energy extension](geant4_proton_compare/LOW_ENERGY_RESULTS.md) exercise native `S2`/CSD, source normalization, table binding and layer electronic energy deposition in thin Al/Cu at 1.2, 2, 5, 10, 100 and 499.99 MeV. Geant4 11.4.2 ionization-only transport is the controlled code comparison. An independent NIST PSTAR electronic-stopping characteristic calculation is available at 1.2, 2 and 5 MeV. At those energies the maximum reported layer difference is 0.355% versus Geant4 and 0.319% versus PSTAR-derived CSDA. These figures are for the defined thin slabs and their selected grids, not measured accuracy or a full proton model.
- In the Geant4-table runs, Radiant uses stopping numbers exported from Geant4. Agreement checks the implementation and scoring with shared input; it does not independently validate the stopping model. The PSTAR comparison breaks that input dependence for electronic stopping, but PSTAR is a stopping/range reference, not another multidimensional proton transport solver.
- The [independent accuracy/speed assessment](PROTON_INDEPENDENT_ACCURACY_SPEED.md) found a 47.94% error in a small flux component on the one-voxel synthetic fixture, reduced to 0.049% by joint refinement. A paper must report grid convergence for each relevant observable, even when total deposition looks stable.
- The [implementation status](PROTON_IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md), [handoff](PROTON_IMPLEMENTATION_HANDOFF.md) and [gate matrix](PROTON_TRANSPORT_GATE_MATRIX.json) keep physical-mode nonelastic transport and secondary production blocked. Straggling, qualified angular scattering, outgoing current/energy closure, exact HTS material data, DPA and activation are not physically validated. A proton energy near the current 1 MeV lower boundary must exit above it for a present-day run; a stopping-target Bragg peak needs a lower cutoff and owned residual-energy accounting.

## Comparator roles and limits

| Comparator | What is independent | Usable test domain | Exclusion/qualification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [NIST PSTAR](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Star/Text/programs.html) | Evaluated electronic/nuclear stopping and CSDA/projected range; published models and measurement-based low-energy fits. | Direct `S(E)` and integrated range checks at 1–500 MeV for listed materials; electronic-only characteristic tests. | It is not a complete transport code. Its tabulated CSDA range uses total stopping, whereas existing slab dose tests intentionally use electronic stopping only. NIST describes about 2–5% uncertainty at 1 MeV, so sub-percent inter-code agreement cannot be interpreted as physical accuracy. |
| [IAEA experimental stopping database](https://www-nds.iaea.org/stopping/) | Individual measured proton stopping datasets with target and publication metadata. | Especially valuable at 1–10 MeV for Al, Cu and any exact HTS constituent/compound with a matching sample state. | Require data-extraction, foil/phase and uncertainty review; do not treat a fit to data as an independent measurement of the same fit. |
| [matRad](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.12251) and [public code](https://github.com/e0404/matRad) | Open analytical pencil-beam dose implementation, separate from Radiant's SN solver. | Water-equivalent clinical pencil beams; published generic proton base data span 31.72–236.1 MeV. Compare selected water/soft-tissue depth dose, range and lateral spread after matching beam model. | It is not a general 1–500 MeV Al/Cu/HTS transport reference. The published two-Gaussian commissioned data used for clinical comparisons were not released. A MATLAB-compatible execution environment and usable generic base data must be verified before running. |
| [Ashby et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-025-02212-1) and [public Python archive v3](https://zenodo.org/records/15425636) | Separately implemented analytical one-dimensional straight-line proton CSD model, with publicly archived source and comparison arrays. | First-priority independent deterministic comparator for homogeneous water fluence/depth dose, especially the paper's 62 MeV, 1% incident-energy-spread case; compare monoenergetic and source-spectrum superposition separately. | The paper assumes no nonelastic production, negligible angular scattering and a homogeneous medium. It uses a Bragg–Kleeman stopping law, so shared-law tests check implementation while native-law tests also expose model differences. The archive page does not establish a redistribution license; local acquisition/execution requires the workspace preflight. |
| [pyRadPlan HongPB](https://pyradplan.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/examples/pencilbeam_proton.html) | Public Python pencil-beam implementation with documented forward engine. | Cross-check a matched water-equivalent single spot in its supported machine-energy grid. | Closely related to the matRad ecosystem and beam data; do not count matRad and pyRadPlan as two independent physics validations without provenance analysis. Their dose kernels do not qualify nuclear residuals/DPA. |
| [KiT-RT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.08417) and [public solver](https://github.com/KiT-RT/kitrt_code) | Separate open C++ finite-volume CSD and angular solvers, including `S_N`, `P_N` and `M_N`; its published validation compares numerical methods and electron therapy cases. | Candidate independent numerical cross-check for manufactured CSD/streaming with an identical synthetic stopping law, after verifying a usable input adapter. | The published irradiation example is for electrons at 20 MeV, not a ready proton benchmark. Re-labeling its particle or sharing a stopping table would validate numerics only, not independent proton physics. Its C++ dependencies require an acquisition/build preflight before a local run. |
| [Bortfeld analytical Bragg curve](https://doi.org/10.1118/1.598116) | Published analytical water depth-dose approximation with a separate derivation. | Shape and range sanity check in its stated therapeutic regime after straggling/cutoff are present. | It is a model, not measured truth and not a secondary-particle or arbitrary-material solution. |
| [Uilkema SN/Fokker–Planck report, 2012](https://research.tudelft.nl/en/publications/proton-therapy-planning-using-the-sn-method-with-the-fokker-planc) | Prior deterministic proton method concept. | Methods and observables to reproduce, if report inputs can be extracted. | Public report metadata alone does not establish runnable code or reusable benchmark data. |
| [Burlacu et al., YODA 2024](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6560/ad6373) | Published deterministic/semi-analytical proton phase-space/dose engine with TOPAS comparisons, including a 100 MeV homogeneous/heterogeneous tank and patient cases. | Excellent methodological analogue for energy straggling, elastic broadening, lateral heterogeneity and a 1%/1 mm gamma analysis. | The paper excludes nuclear interactions, extracts stopping powers from TOPAS, and no public runnable YODA release was established in this review. Its physics-input comparison is not independent of TOPAS. |
| [Vassiliev and Mohan 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24514) | Published deterministic Boltzmann design and Geant4 water comparisons at 40, 100, 160, 220 MeV with dose and fluence spectra. | Literature comparison for spectra, nuclear treatment, error reporting and runtime definitions. | The article labels its method patent-protected; no open runnable implementation is established. It cannot satisfy the independent-code run requirement at present. |
| [NASA HZETRN2020](https://software.nasa.gov/software/LAR-19979-1) | A different deterministic shielding transport family with proton-induced secondaries. | Possible later nuclear/shielding comparison after obtaining and auditing the release package and matched observables. | Its General Public Release is request-based, not an established installed/open-source comparator; arbitrary-layer, isotope-resolved output and license fitness remain open. |
| Geant4 11.4.2 | Independent Monte Carlo transport algorithm; can be controlled with matching switches or used with a separately justified physical physics list. | 1–500 MeV, thin to thick, layered materials, secondary and recoil tallies when configured and converged. | A Monte Carlo comparison is a code comparison, not measurement validation. Separate stochastic SE, step/cut settings and hadronic-model uncertainty. |

## Acceptance design and paper sequence

1. **Freeze input and observable definitions.** For each row record material formulation, state, density, isotope fractions, source energy/angle/position distribution, score normalization, process toggles, energy cutoff, Geant4 physics list and step/production cut. Pre-register grids, measurement uncertainty and comparison metrics before running final validation cases. A reviewer should be able to reproduce every plotted point from retained scripts and raw scores.
2. **Separate three error sources and three independence axes.** Vary Radiant spatial/group/angular resolution, Geant4 step/cuts/statistics and physics models independently. Report separately whether a case has an independent software implementation, independent physical inputs and independent experimental evidence. The total residual is not an estimate of Radiant error if the reference itself changes under those variations. For measured data use uncertainty-weighted residuals and source/detector covariance. For curves report absolute normalization, range/peak/distal landmarks, pointwise residuals and a pre-specified low-dose mask. Gamma analysis may supplement these metrics but cannot replace them.
3. **First paper go/no-go:** a defensible bounded title is *Verification and controlled electromagnetic benchmarking of one-dimensional deterministic proton slowing-down transport in Radiant.jl* if V1/V1b, P1, C1 and an independent implementation such as D0 replicate with archived data. An independent stopping input must be reported separately from shared-input verification. Call a specific stopping observable *validated against experiment* only after matching and comparing a measured dataset with its uncertainty. Thin-slab Geant4/PSTAR agreement alone supports a code-benchmark claim. Do not use “validated 1–500 MeV irradiation”, “Bragg peak”, “full Geant4 agreement” or “DPA prediction” in this paper while physical-mode guard remains blocked.
4. **Full-model go/no-go:** require C2, D1, E1/E2, N1 and H1 in their supported energy/material domain, with measured/independent evidence for each claimed process. In particular, a 200–500 MeV pion-capable event producer, outgoing-current/energy closure and residual/secondary ownership must be qualified before a complete 1–500 MeV claim. If an independent deterministic code cannot be run on the exact problem, report the gap; matRad/pyRadPlan clinical comparisons cannot fill it.

## Further primary evidence to examine

- [Wieser et al., matRad, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.12251) documents 2%/2 mm gamma comparisons to clinical planning systems; that result applies to its commissioned beam data, and the published two-Gaussian base data were not released.
- [Luomajärvi, 1979](https://doi.org/10.1080/00337577908237920) measured proton stopping in Al and Cu among other metals at 0.3–1.5 MeV, with reported absolute uncertainties of 1.5–2.5%. [Ishiwari et al., 1988](https://doi.org/10.1016/0168-583X(88)90482-X) measured Al/Cu stopping at 3–9 MeV with stated ±0.4% accuracy. Their numerical values and sample details must be accessed or traced in the IAEA collection before using them as quantitative validation rows.
- [Bortfeld, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1118/1.598116) gives an analytical water Bragg-curve approximation, useful as a shape check within its assumptions.
- [Ashby et al., 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00285-025-02212-1) derive a homogeneous straight-line proton characteristic solution using a Bragg–Kleeman range law, compare 62 MeV water calculations with MCsquare/TOPAS, and provide a [small public Python archive](https://zenodo.org/records/15425636) with code and comparison arrays. The model explicitly omits nonelastic production and meaningful angular deflection; its archived source needs an environment/license audit before execution or redistribution.
- [KiT-RT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.08417) is a public CSD/SN finite-volume numerical comparator, but its published therapy example is a 20 MeV electron beam. It is a candidate for shared synthetic-law solver verification only, not an already validated proton engine.
- [Gottschalk et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1088/0031-9155/60/14/5627) distinguish electromagnetic core, charged-secondary halo and neutral aura in measured 177 MeV water pencil beams. [Hall et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.08154) compare Geant4 nuclear models against that measurement and identify entrance-dose/source limitations.
- [Testa et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1118/1.4828781) validate TOPAS/Geant4 against measured spread-out Bragg peaks, absolute output, lateral profiles behind a half-beam shifter, and multilayer Faraday-cup charge. The mix of observables is a useful end-to-end design precedent, but its clinical source/nozzle and reference code differ from our target.
- [IAEA selected charged-particle cross-section database](https://nucleus.iaea.org/Pages/charged-particle-cross-section-database.aspx) covers selected evaluated reactions only up to 100 MeV; it cannot establish 200–500 MeV event production.
- [IAEA EXFOR](https://nucleus.iaea.org/Pages/experimental-nuclear-reaction-data.aspx) includes measured charged-particle reaction records. [Michel and Otuka's 2023 compilation](https://nds.iaea.org/publications/indc/indc-ger-0052rev.pdf) tabulates proton-induced residual-production cross sections through 2.6 GeV for selected elements including Al and Cu. These are candidates for independent high-energy residual-yield tests, subject to exact reaction, isotope, uncertainty and target-state checks.
- The [IAEA spallation benchmark experiment index](https://www-nds.iaea.org/spallations/spal_exp.html) already names exact 160–500 MeV differential and residual-production cases and EXFOR IDs, making N2 a concrete later test. The index is an inventory, not a downloaded numerical benchmark or a validation of any current Geant4 physics list.
- [Jia et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4821440) irradiated YBCO coated conductors with 4 MeV protons and measured critical-current response. This is a relevant specimen/beam precedent, but critical current is not a direct DPA or recoil-spectrum measurement. [Nordlund et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-03415-5) distinguish displacement/replacement damage estimators; the damage model and REBCO material-response assumptions must be stated explicitly.
- [DoseRAD2026 dataset paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12778) describes paired beam-level Geant4/Monte Carlo dose data and a public analytical pencil-beam baseline. The [official dataset page](https://doserad2026.grand-challenge.org/data/) lists an 864 GB dataset, public training cases and private test cases; its 31.7–200.8 MeV beamlets cannot cover the low- or high-energy scope here. It is a possible later clinical case, not an immediate acquisition or a second independent experiment.
- [Schellhammer et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.12961) measured field-induced trajectory and Bragg-peak displacement for 80–180 MeV proton pencil beams in a 0.95 T PMMA setup. It is a candidate only after Radiant has the matching geometry and source model.
